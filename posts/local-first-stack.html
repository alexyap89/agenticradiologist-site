A local-first AI stack for a radiologist

By Alexander Yap · 21 Apr 2026

“Radiologist runs an AI stack at home” is either a clickbait article or it is this one, and I intend it to be this one. So: the actual stack, the honest numbers, and why a person who signs radiology reports makes the conservative choice.

Why local, in three sentences

First, data: patient data is a trust boundary, not a cost optimisation, and the only boundary that cannot be breached is the one where the data never left the building. Second, audit: I can read the code of my own agents and I know exactly where the weights came from. Third, and this is the one I keep returning to — the cloud is a subscription to someone else’s agent. Someone else’s priorities, someone else’s pricing, someone else’s right to change the model under you while you sleep.

The stack

hardware:    one workstation, 24 GB VRAM (a large Mac works too)
inference:   llama.cpp via Ollama, 7–14B instruct models
memory:      SQLite + sqlite-vec. Nothing fancier.
agents:      plain Python, one file each, git for state
interface:   CLI first, a messaging app second

That is the whole diagram. There is no cloud tier, no managed vector database, no SSO. The “enterprise architecture” I was expected to draw in 2023 is now four lines, and I find that funny in a way that I will get over.

The honest numbers

A 14B model on 24 GB of VRAM gives me somewhere around 10–30 tokens per second depending on context length. That is not fast. It is private, auditable, and — after the hardware purchase — roughly free at the margin. Latency is a feature you design for once you stop pretending the model is instantaneous: batch work overnight, use small models for triage, save the big one for the two or three jobs a week that earn it.

Capability, also honestly: strong at summarisation and structure, serviceable at code, still embarrassingly bad at counting and at exact numbers. So it drafts and I check, with exactly the same standard I would apply to a junior registrar. It is a colleague with a particular skill profile, not an oracle.

What “local-first” actually means

It is not a technical position; it is an ownership question. Who runs the model, who sees the data, who is accountable when it is wrong? In medicine those three answers should be you. Everything else — the models, the frameworks, the hype cycle — is negotiable. Local-first is what remains when you stop negotiating.
